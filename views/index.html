      Frequently Asked Questions
      
        
      Quick answers about how Link Guard analyzes links.
      
      
      

      
      
        
      
          How does Link Guard detect zero-day phishing without external blacklists?
          
      
            Traditional blacklist databases take 24–72 hours to record newly launched malicious sites. Link Guard inspects the fundamental properties of the domain: registration date via RDAP, TLS handshake certificates, raw IP presence, and homograph character codes. This flags fraudulent sites the moment they go live.
          
      
        
      

        
      
          What is an IDN Homograph attack?
          
      
            Attackers register domains using international characters that look identical to standard Latin characters (for example, using the Cyrillic 'а' in place of the Latin 'a'). When translated by web browsers into Punycode (xn--...), the browser loads the attacker's server. Link Guard inspects Punycode domains and flags them instantly.
          
      
        
      

        
      
          Why are raw IP addresses considered suspicious?
          
      
            Legitimate services, banks, and enterprises operate on branded domain names. Attackers often use direct IP addresses (e.g. http://192.168.1.1/login) to bypass domain registration costs, evade domain reputation filters, and quickly rotate compromised servers.
          
      
        
      

        
      
          Does Link Guard store my scanned links?
          
      
            No. Link Guard processes requests in memory with local heuristics. Scan results are displayed directly to your client session and saved only in your local browser storage for your personal scan history.
